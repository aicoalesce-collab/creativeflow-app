CreativeFlow · redesign proposal

Right now it shows you everything. It should tell you one thing.

The build is sound and the palette has character. What it lacks is a point of view about what matters most on each screen — so every element competes at the same volume, and the person opening it has to do the sorting themselves.

Nothing is built yet. The mockups below are live HTML in the proposed system, not pictures — resize the window and they respond. Type here is a system stand-in; the real build keeps Doto and Inter.

01

What's holding it back

Five things, from going through all 39 plates. Each is fixable without touching the backend.

01

The dashboard has no first thing plate 03

Five identical KPI cards, an overdue strip, a task list with its own tabs, a notifications column and a workload chart — all the same weight. Nothing is bigger, darker or quieter than anything else, so the eye has nowhere to land. Five equal numbers means none of them is important.

02

The accent has five jobs throughout

Orange is the brand, every primary button, the overdue strip, the review badge and a campaign colour. When the loud colour is everywhere, nothing reads as loud. Overdue work has to shout over the Save button.

03

Status is written, not shown plates 04, 11

A row's state lives in a word-chip you have to read. Scanning twenty rows for what's stuck means reading twenty labels. State should be visible before it is legible.

04

The task dialog is a form, not a workspace plate 30

Twelve fields and eight actions in one centred box. The things people change constantly — status, the file, a note — sit at the same level as team and due time, which almost never change after creation. It is the densest surface in the product and the best single thing to redo.

05

Eight slots in the phone bar plates 34–39

Dashboard, Tasks, Review, Assigners, Projects, Gallery, Calendar, Reports. Three of those are the same list sliced differently. At 390px each label is 7.5px — technically fitting, not comfortably.

02

The idea

One clear answer, then the detail. Every screen picks a single thing it is for, gives that thing real weight, and lets everything else recede. On the dashboard that answer is "what is late or due today". In a task it is "what is its state and where is the file". In a campaign it is "how much is left".

Concretely, that means spending contrast rather than spreading it: one loud element per screen, a strict accent, and density that varies with urgency instead of being uniform.

03

The system

An evolution of what's there, not a replacement — the orange stays, it just stops being everywhere. The ground warms slightly and lightens, so white cards actually read as white.

The rule that does the work: orange only ever means "this needs you" or "this is the action". State gets its own quiet set, so a red-orange row is genuinely late rather than merely present.

Type. Keep Doto — but restrict it to the wordmark and to numerals, where a pixel face is genuinely good: KPI figures, task IDs, timecodes, dates. Everything you actually read stays in one sans. Today Doto also sets campaign names and view titles at uppercase 21px, which is where it becomes hard work.

Density. Three levels instead of one. Late work gets a heavier row and a solid bar; ordinary work sits neutral; finished work fades to about half strength and stops competing.

04

Dashboard

The same information, re-ranked. One urgent block at the top, then numbers as a quiet strip rather than five cards, then the day's work. The workload chart moves to Reports, where someone is actually asking that question.

08

What I would not touch

  • The orange. It's yours, it's recognisable, it works. It just needs a smaller job.
  • Doto. Genuinely distinctive. Restricted to the wordmark and numbers it becomes a signature instead of a texture.
  • The review room. The best-designed screen in the product already — media left, conversation right, pins on the artwork. It's the model the rest should follow.
  • Dark mode. Already properly token-driven, so the whole proposal comes along for free.
  • Everything behind the glass. No backend change, no schema change, no migration. This is markup and CSS.
09

If you like it

I'd do it in four passes, each shippable on its own, so you can stop at any point and still be better off than now.

  • Pass 1 — tokens. New palette, the accent rule, the three density levels, state bars. One file, every screen changes at once. Half a day, and it's the biggest visible jump.
  • Pass 2 — dashboard. Re-rank into the urgent block, stat strip and two panes.
  • Pass 3 — task panel. Modal becomes a side sheet with folded details.
  • Pass 4 — navigation. Only if you want it. The riskiest, because it changes where people expect things to be.

What I need from you

  1. Does the proposed dashboard read better than the current one? That's the whole thesis in one screen.
  2. Eight tabs or four?
  3. Anything here you actively dislike — easier to hear now than after it's built.
