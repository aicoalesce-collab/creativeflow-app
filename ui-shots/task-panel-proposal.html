  • A head sending work back at QC does not burn a revision round. Only the assigner's changes count against the three-round limit. Your internal check is free, which is the right way round.
  • The head is copied automatically when the assigner requests changes — that already happens, you don't have to tell them.
  • Rounds are numbered and carried. Round 2 goes back through the head, and the assigner is pinged again with a fresh review link each time it passes QC.
  • One difference from your description: when a head submits their own work it skips QC and goes straight to the assigner — they would only be checking themselves. Say the word if you want it to stop for a second head instead.

What I'd change: the member's action currently reads Status → In Review. It becomes one button that says Send for QC →, so nobody has to know that "In Review" means "my head has it".

04

What goes, and where it goes

On screen today, always

DeletePass QC → requesterReviewReject Approve → DoneRequest revisionsCloseSave changes

Eight, of which at most two ever apply. Six are decisions you cannot make, shown at the same weight as the one you can.

Where each one lands

  • Delete, Reject → behind ···. Both are rare and neither is reversible.
  • Save changes → gone. Fields save when you leave them, like the sheet does.
  • Close → gone. Escape, or click outside.
  • Review → becomes the dark primary button, but only when there is something to review.
  • Pass QC / Approve / Request revisions → one pair, only for whoever's move it is.

The twelve fields fold under Details. Team, priority, due time and brief almost never change after a task is created — and when they do, it is deliberate enough to be worth one click.

05

Assigned by, and who allocates

Two people, side by side at the top of every task: who asked for it and who is doing it. It's the first thing anyone wants to know, and today it sits in grey subtitle text between the created date and the revision count.

Assigned by fills itself — it is whoever created the task, never a field anyone picks. The label changes with the state, because the useful fact changes: Assigned by normally, Submitted by while a head is doing QC, Changes from during a revision round.

And the allocation rule you just asked for: when an assigner sends work in, they do not choose who does it. The task arrives with the team head, who allocates. So the new-task box is a different shape depending on who opens it.
